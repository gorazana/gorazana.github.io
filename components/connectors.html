Where this component stops

Not Defender login, OAuth authorization-server implementation, universal ETL tool, unscoped credential vault, anonymous API proxy or assumption that every provider supports refresh/idempotency.

Model & lifecycle

Typed contracts, owned persistence and configuration are preserved in SOURCE_REFERENCE.md, original sections 5–6. Adapt to the actual implementation; these are not applied migrations.

Authorized principal starts connection for allowed provider/scopes → persist hashed state and protected PKCE verifier → exact redirect → verify one-time callback and issuer/account → exchange credentials and record consent. Refresh acquires current generation lease, invokes provider once, then CAS stores successor tokens. A timeout after rotating refresh is uncertain and may require provider recovery/reconnect; do not keep replaying an invalidated old token. External actions resolve current scope/consent and typed adapter, enforce rate budget and persist outcome/next cursor only after durable accepted result. ### Do not duplicate social authentication Signing in with Google identifies a principal and belongs to Defender's identity providers. Granting an application permission to synchronize a Google calendar is an external connection, which may be a different account and requires separate consent, scopes and revocation. Sharing a redirect handler without preserving these distinctions risks account linking and confused-deputy errors. Document them separately in examples.

Operational requirements

One service connection with host keyring and native queue; provider choice explicit. Optional expanded profile: Many scoped connections, delegated admin/consent, token rotation, synchronization workers and provider-specific rate management.
