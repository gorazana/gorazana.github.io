Build patterns

Compose the parts. Keep the whole.

A few honest sketches of how small native parts fit into real applications. These are illustrative compositions, not shipped products, and most of the parts named are still research.

Ways to compose

Start from a shape you recognise.

Each sketch names parts that already appear in the catalog. Open any card there to see its contract, boundaries and current status before you plan around it.

Open the catalog

An API product

Identity and scoped authorization behind a focused set of modules, served over HTTP with no interface you did not ask for.

A multi-tenant application

Host-admitted tenants with isolated resources and guarded database placement, over a shared identity and trust foundation.

A publishing platform

Structured content and media with roles and review, composed from content modules rather than one monolithic engine.

Commerce and fulfillment

Catalog, orders and invoicing as separate modules that agree on clear contracts, so each can be reasoned about on its own.

An education system

Admissions, learning and assessments as distinct parts, each owning its records and leaving policy to the host.

Offline field operations

Logistics, devices and sync shaped for intermittent connectivity, with state and recovery made explicit.

A collaborative workspace

Realtime delivery and shared editing as bounded services you can place behind your own experience.

Manufacturing and logistics

Inventory, manufacturing and shipments that exchange facts through tested ports, not shared internals.

People and recruitment

Workforce, recruitment and timekeeping as modules that keep human-led decisions in the host's hands.

A service or nonprofit portal

Cases, fundraising and impact composed into a portal that stays small and legible.

Illustrative relationships, not an executable starter. Availability, access and version are decided per component and shown on its own page.
